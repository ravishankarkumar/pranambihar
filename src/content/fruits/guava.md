Guava is less ceremonial than some celebrated fruits, but that everyday familiarity is precisely why it belongs here. It appears in orchards, markets, school-time snacks and roadside carts, eaten at different stages of ripeness and often sharpened with salt or spice.

The next layer of this entry is varietal and regional: which guavas people in different districts look for, when each crop tastes best and how orchard practice is changing.
