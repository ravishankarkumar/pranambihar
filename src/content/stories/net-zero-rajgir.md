The new Nalanda University campus in Rajgir describes itself as net-zero. That phrase becomes meaningful only when it is opened up into systems people can see, measure and question.

The university documents a large solar installation, biogas capacity, extensive water bodies, landscape works, earth-block construction and water management influenced by the regional ahar-pyne tradition. Together, these choices present the campus as an experiment in combining contemporary engineering with knowledge suited to its landscape.

## Beyond an architectural photograph

Sustainability coverage often stops at a dramatic building image. A better account follows energy, water, waste, heat and maintenance. How does a system perform across seasons? What does it require from the people operating it? How comfortable and accessible are the spaces for students and staff? Which lessons can travel to other institutions, and which depend on this particular site?

## The public test

Pranam Bihar will treat the campus as the first subject in a recurring series about public and institutional design. The method will combine published specifications with observation and informed review. Institutional claims will be attributed; measured results will be distinguished from design intent.

The most useful story is not that a modern campus exists in Bihar. It is that an institution in Bihar is attempting a specific response to energy, water and climate—and that its performance can teach us something.
