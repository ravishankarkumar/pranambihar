Magahi is rooted in the Magadh region of central and southern Bihar. Its principal geography includes Patna, Jehanabad, Nalanda, Nawada, Gaya and Sheikhpura, with speech continuing into neighbouring districts.

## A language landscape, not a hard border

To the west, Magahi gradually meets Bhojpuri; toward the east it meets Angika, while varieties related to Maithili are heard across the Ganga. These transitions remind us that linguistic regions overlap rather than ending neatly at administrative lines.

## Everyday life and public visibility

Magahi remains a language of homes, fields, neighbourhoods, performance and oral knowledge. Yet speakers may identify their mother tongue as Hindi in official contexts. Future work should document contemporary literature, theatre, folk performance and how the language moves between rural life, towns and digital media.
