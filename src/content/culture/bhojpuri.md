Bhojpuri is rooted in western Bihar and extends across eastern Uttar Pradesh, parts of Jharkhand and the Terai of Nepal. In Bihar it is widely associated with Bhojpur, Buxar, Rohtas, Kaimur, Saran, Siwan, Gopalganj and the Champaran districts, with speech changing gradually across regional boundaries.

## A language that travelled

Migration carried Bhojpuri far beyond the subcontinent. Its global story now includes communities whose ancestors travelled as indentured labourers as well as newer generations moving between Bihar, Indian cities and countries around the world. Songs, films and digital media give the language enormous reach, but popular culture is only one part of its life.

## Script and recognition

Kaithi has an important historical relationship with written Bhojpuri; Devanagari is more commonly used today. Bhojpuri is not currently included in the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution of India, and campaigns for constitutional and official recognition remain part of its contemporary public story.

The fuller archive must hold literature, theatre, oral history and ordinary household speech alongside cinema and music.
