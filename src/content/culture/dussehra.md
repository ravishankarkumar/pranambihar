Dussehra is not only its final day. Across the Navratri and Durga Puja period, temples, pandals, fairs, fasting, food and neighbourhood journeys create a ten-day rhythm that differs from one part of Bihar to another.

In some Bihari families, the period is also called **Dassai**. Children are discouraged from wandering outside during particular hours because this is believed to be a spiritually unsettled time in which harmful spirits may hurt them. This is a living household belief—not a universal rule—and belongs in the record alongside the festival’s more visible celebrations.

The protective dimension also appears in regional performance. Jhijhiya traditions in Mithila connect dance, lamp-lit pots, songs and ideas of protection during the Navratri–Dussehra season. The festival therefore holds public devotion and mela culture together with quieter rules remembered at home.
